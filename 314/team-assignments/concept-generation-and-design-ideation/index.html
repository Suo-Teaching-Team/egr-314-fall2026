    Assignment

    Note: This assignment will be posted to your team's living report website on Github

    Step 1: Background reading

    1. Review the Course Sequence Requirementsto ensure you are familiar with the particular parameters of this semesters's project.

    2. See the "Brainstorm Techniques" links above for good ideas on how to generate more ideas.

      These two articles share more information on successful ideation techniques and data on the effectiveness of ideation and brainstorming.

    Step 2: Learn about Wildfire Response

    In two paragraphs, describe:

    1. What is the goal of your project in terms of Wildfire Response?
    2. Who is your audience?

    Step 3: Generate Ideas

    Perform a product brainstorm to generate a large number of features, concepts, and ideas about what your product COULD be. Use sticky notes, a whiteboard, chalkboard, or an online whiteboard / slide tool, to generate ~100 or so brief concepts / features / ideas for your product.

    During your Brainstorm, think of solutions to the following questions, derived from the readings:

    1. What kinds of cues will you provide to make the use of your device easier?

    2. What role will durability, safety, and comfort play in the user experience?

    3. What kind of instruction will be needed to use the device?

    There are some basic rules to brainstorming, spelled out here:

    • Don't criticize any ideas.
    • Encourage a wide variety of ideas.
    • Build on each other's ideas.
    • Don't be afraid to share unrealistic or unconventional ideas.

    Take a snapshot of your board here at this point before you start to rearrange it.

    Step 4: Sort, Rank, and Group

    Some of the most effective phases of ideation come after the brainstorm session. Seeing innovative patterns in the noise, grouping features in ways other products don't, and re-mixing good ideas into better ideas is often just as effective as coming up with many disjointed ideas. Take some time to discuss your brainstorm.

    Take a snapshot of your board here at key points in this process.

    1. Sort your features / ideas thematically. Maybe some ideas are functions. Perhaps others are strategies for keeping attention; maybe others are user interface ideas. Regardless, group them along the different themes that emerge from the brainstorm.
    2. Spend some time ranking top ideas. You can star good ideas, or simply put them at the top of their group.
    3. Recombine and regenerate new ideas from the ones that you like the most.
    4. Start grouping the best ideas together into your "killer app"

    Take a snapshot of your board here at this point.

    Step 5: Create a concept sketches (or similar) for your project

    Develop one visually-engaging representation for a project concept that uses the top brainstormed concepts identified in the previous step. The purpose of this concept is to highlight the features grouped into each concept developed above. Possible representations include:

    • Physical cardboard / wood / 3-D printed / etc. mockup

      • Take photos of your mockup.
      • Use a vector based drawing program to annotate your photos with text boxes and arrows to highlight all features included in this design concept.

      See What is a Vector Image? and Suggested Drawing Software on the Embedded Systems Design website.

    • A detailed, computer-generated, vector-based sketch of the Wildfire Response concept

      • Annotate your photos with text boxes and arrows to highlight all features included in this design concept.
      • No hand-drawn sketches. If you decide to use CAD, do not just produce a dimensioned engineering drawing. The goal is to highlight the features and functionality of your Project

      See What is a Vector Image? and Suggested Drawing Software on the Embedded Systems Design website.

    • Visual story-based representation of how your team's design will be used by a user (user experience). Examples include (but not limited to):

      • Computer-generated, vector-based annotated storyboard, comic strip, or graphic novel. Make sure to have at least one human character in the story. No hand-drawn sketches.
      • Narrated stop motion animation video telling the story of the product in a single short (<5 minutes) video.
      • Mock print/video advertisement or infomercial for the device and how it solves the problems of the user.

      As with other representations, ensure all features and ideas included with the product concept are included in the story, video, ad, etc.

    • Other form of representation (with prior approval from your instructor).

    Describe, in one page (in word, single spaced, 12pt times new roman font),

    • How the features highlighted in your concept sketch satisfy the needs of Wildfire response.

    • How the functionality will will be split across four teammates (See the project description)

      • Internet-based two-way communication
      • Human machine interface via a screen and buttons
      • Sensor and Actuator-based controlled response

    • What kinds of cues will you provide to make the use of your device easier?

    • How do you plan on designing your "controls"
    • What role will durability, safety, and comfort play in the user experience?
    • What kind of instruction will be needed to use the device?

    Step 6: Presentation

    1. Create a five-minute powerpoint(roughly 1 slide / minute):

      1. Team Organization
        1. Team members
        2. Charter
        3. Mission Statement
      2. Wildfire Response Summary
        1. Overview of what you learned from the talk/visit
        2. What is the goal of your device?
        3. Who is your audience?
      3. Design Ideation
        1. Organization of your brainstorm session
        2. Screenshot of your "board" mid-process
        3. Final layout of your sorted, ranked, and refined concepts
      4. Overview of your design concept "sketch"
        1. Annotated figure from step 4, plus feature walkthrough
        2. Summary of your answers from the passage you wrote.

    2. Practice your presentation to ensure you meet the general length requirements for the video.

    3. Record your presentation and save as an .mp4. See the resources above for tutorials.
    4. Upload to Youtube. You may set the video to unlisted or public, but do not set it to private.
    5. Embed in your team's website. Once uploaded, go to the video page on youtube and click "share". Select "embed" from the sharing options and copy the html code. This code can be pasted directly into the markdown file for this assignment. > Note: Please refrain from uploading videos to Canvas or sharing links to files on google drive. Videos must be embedded on your website via YouTube.

    Homework Preparation and Submission

    This work will be used -- and graded -- in multiple ways. It will be checked for completeness on the date given in Canvas.

    Preparation

    Please prepare this assignment as a new page on your team's living report website. Assemble:

    • Your response from part 2
    • The many phases of your brainstorm session
      • initial capture of design features
      • during and after sorting, ranking and, refinement
      • features grouped into your single design concept
    • Design concept sketch
    • 1-page rationale
    • embedded youtube video presentation

    Assemble this into a new page on your team's website called "Ideation and Concept Generation"

    Once completed,

    • Create a link to this new page on the datasheet's main landing page.
    • Check the link to ensure that it works
    • export this new page as a pdf
    • submit the pdf and link to Canvas

    The new page representing your assignment should not present as a page of links. Do not use it to link to other documents especially living documents, such as google docs, draw.io drawings, or google sheets. Rather, contents from other documents should be exported, saved in your repository, and hosted natively in markdown (or if need be html) on the site itself.

    Submission

    To submit a complete assignment, please submit:

    • A working URL to the new "Ideation and Concept Generation" page.
    • the exported PDF for easier review

    Both items must be submitted, by the deadline in the Canvas course calendar, in order to receive full credit. It is your responsibility to ensure that your submission to Canvas was successful. Late Canvas submissions will be graded per the policy in the syllabus.
