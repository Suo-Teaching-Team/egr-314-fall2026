### Step 3: Generate Ideas

Perform a product brainstorm to generate a large number of features, concepts, and ideas about what your product *COULD* be. Use sticky notes, a whiteboard, chalkboard, or an online whiteboard / slide tool, to generate ~20 or so brief concepts / features / ideas for your product.

During your Brainstorm, think of solutions to the following questions, derived from the readings:

1. What kinds of cues will you provide to make the use of your device easier?

1. What role will durability, safety, and comfort play in the user experience?
1. What kind of instruction will be needed to use the device? 


There are some basic rules to brainstorming, spelled out [here](https://lucidspark.com/blog/4-group-brainstorming-techniques):

* Don't criticize any ideas.
* Encourage a wide variety of ideas.
* Build on each other's ideas.
* Don't be afraid to share unrealistic or unconventional ideas.

> Take a snapshot of your board here at this point before you start to rearrange it.

### Step 4: Sort, Rank, and Group

Some of the most effective phases of ideation come ***after*** the brainstorm session. Seeing innovative patterns in the noise, grouping features in ways other products don't, and re-mixing good ideas into better ideas is often just as effective as coming up with many disjointed ideas. Take some time to discuss your brainstorm.

> Take a snapshot of your board here at key points in this process.

1. Sort your features / ideas thematically.  Maybe some ideas are functions.  Perhaps others are strategies for keeping attention; maybe others are user interface ideas.  Regardless, group them along the different themes that emerge from the brainstorm.
1. Spend some time ranking top ideas.  You can star good ideas, or simply put them at the top of their group.
1. Recombine and regenerate new ideas from the ones that you like the most.
1. Start grouping the best ideas together into your "killer app"

> Take a snapshot of your board here at this point.

### Step 5: Create a concept sketches (or similar) for your project

Develop one visually-engaging representation for a project concept that uses the top brainstormed concepts identified in the previous step.  The purpose of this concept is to highlight the features grouped into each concept developed above. Possible representations include:

* Physical cardboard / wood / 3-D printed / etc. mockup
    * Take photos of your mockup.
    * Use a **vector based drawing program** to annotate your photos with text boxes and arrows to highlight ***all*** features included in this design concept.

    > See [What is a Vector Image?](https://vimm.com/what-is-a-vector-image/) and [Suggested Drawing Software](https://embedded-systems-design.github.io/drawing-software/) on the Embedded Systems Design website.

* A detailed, ***computer-generated, vector-based sketch*** of the Wildfire Response concept
    * Annotate your photos with text boxes and arrows to highlight ***all*** features included in this design concept.
    * **No hand-drawn sketches.**  If you decide to use CAD, do not just produce a dimensioned engineering drawing.  The goal is to highlight the features and functionality of your Project

    > See [What is a Vector Image?](https://vimm.com/what-is-a-vector-image/) and [Suggested Drawing Software](https://embedded-systems-design.github.io/drawing-software/) on the Embedded Systems Design website.

* Visual *story-based* representation of how your team's design will be used by a user (user experience). Examples include (but not limited to):
    * Computer-generated, vector-based annotated storyboard, comic strip, or graphic novel. Make sure to have at least one human character in the story. **No hand-drawn sketches.**
    * Narrated [stop motion animation video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8qxLPUOMLJY) telling the story of the product in a single short (<5 minutes) video.
    * Mock print/video advertisement or infomercial for the device and how it solves the problems of the user.

    > As with other representations, ensure ***all*** features and ideas included with the product concept are included in the story, video, ad, etc.

* Other form of representation (*with prior approval* from your instructor).

Describe, in one page (in word, single spaced, 12pt times new roman font),  

* How the features highlighted in your concept sketch satisfy the needs of Wildfire response.  
* How the functionality will will be split across four teammates  (See the project description)
    * Internet-based two-way communication
    * Human machine interface via a screen and buttons
    * Sensor and Actuator-based controlled response

1. How do you plan on designing your "controls"
